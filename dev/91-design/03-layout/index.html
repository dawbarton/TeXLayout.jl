Math layout

Layout turns an AST into a flat list of positioned elements. It is a recursive walk: _layout_node! in src/layout.jl dispatches on NodeKind to a _layout_xxx! helper, which appends LayoutBox values to one shared vector and returns the node's horizontal advance. The helpers live by topic in src/layout/scripts.jl, constructs.jl, extensible.jl, and matrix.jl, and the lookup tables they use in src/tables/. The TeXbook rule behind each construct, with the MATH constants it reads, is in katex_rules.md.

Output

abstract type TeXElement end

struct Glyph <: TeXElement        # found by name in a font slot
    glyph_name::String
    font_slot::FontSlot.T
    advance_width::Int
    left_side_bearing::Int
    x_min::Int; y_min::Int; x_max::Int; y_max::Int
end

struct GlyphID <: TeXElement      # an exact glyph of an exact font file
    glyph_id::UInt32
    font_path::String
    font_slot::FontSlot.T
    represented_char::Char
    advance_width::Int
    left_side_bearing::Int
    x_min::Int; y_min::Int; x_max::Int; y_max::Int
end

struct HRule <: TeXElement; width::Float64; thickness::Float64; end   # em
struct VRule <: TeXElement; height::Float64; thickness::Float64; end  # em
struct Space <: TeXElement; width::Float64; end                       # em

struct LayoutBox
    element::TeXElement
    x::Float64       # em, right of the formula origin
    y::Float64       # em, above the baseline
    scale::Float64   # size relative to the base font size
end

Glyph metrics are in the font's design units; du * scale / upm gives em. font_slot names the font a Glyph comes from: FontSlot.Math for math glyphs, or Regular, Bold, Italic, or BoldItalic for text, each falling back through the family's other slots and finally the math font. A renderer resolves a Glyph through that whole chain (GlyphRuntime, see Renderer adapters). A GlyphID needs no resolution: it comes from a shaper, and its glyph may be a ligature or substitution with no Unicode scalar or name of its own.

HRule.y is the rule's bottom edge and VRule.x its left edge; the adapters convert to the line centres their hosts expect.

Coordinates

The origin is the formula's left end on its baseline; x runs right and y up, both in em. scale is 1 at the top level, about 0.7 in a script, and about 0.5 in a second-level script; the exact values come from the font (size_scale). To draw at a font size of fs pixels, multiply x and y by fs.

The layout context

Every recursive call receives the same immutable _LayoutCtx:

struct _LayoutCtx
    family::FontFamily
    mt::MathTable          # the math font's MATH table
    upm::Float64
    mode::LayoutMode.T     # Math, or Text inside \text{…}
    font_variant::Symbol   # :default, or the alphabet of an enclosing \mathbf, \mathbb, …
    text_shaper::TextShaper
end

_LayoutCtx(family, shaper) builds a math-mode context, and _with(ctx; mode, font_variant) is the only copy helper. _with_variant (for FontSwitch), _with_text_mode (for \text{…}: upright glyphs, no math spacing, and no math alphabet, as in LaTeX), and _with_math_mode (for $…$ inside \text{…}) are written with it. The changed context flows into every nested call, so \mathbf{x_i} sets both letters in bold.

Range emission

A construct that must measure a child before placing it (scripts, fractions, radicals, delimiters, accents, braces, matrices) lays the child out at a temporary origin straight into the output vector, records the (start, stop) range it emitted, measures that range (_boxes_top, _boxes_bottom), and then moves it into place with _translate_range!. A helper may only move ranges it has just emitted. Some constructs emit their children before their own rules or delimiters, so append order is not paint order.

Glyphs

Characters and symbols are found by codepoint (see the invariants): _char_glyph for letters, digits, and punctuation, after _MATH_CHAR_REMAP (hyphen to minus, * to ∗); _cmd_glyph for commands, through _SYMBOL_CODEPOINTS; _upright_glyph for operator names and text, from the regular font when there is one; and _variant_glyph for math alphabets. Large operators use _DISPLAY_OP_CODEPOINTS and take their display size from the font's vertical variants (display_operator_min_height).

A math character is followed by its MATH-table italic correction, emitted as a Space kern (_push_math_char!), as in TeX's make_ord; between letters of a text alphabet (\mathit, \mathrm, …) _drop_italic_kern! removes it again. In Script and ScriptScript styles, characters, symbols, and large operators switch to the font's ssty alternates (_script_glyph), as unicode-math does.

Atoms and spacing

_atom_class gives each node one of TeX's classes (:ord, :op, :bin, :rel, :open, :close, :punct, :inner): characters from _CHAR_ATOM_CLASS, commands from _CMD_ATOM_CLASS (both in src/tables/layout_atoms.jl), and other kinds by rule. A scripted node takes its base's class, a font switch its body's, and an explicit Space is transparent. Fractions, \binom, and undelimited matrices are Ord, as \frac wraps its fraction in braces; \left…\right, delimited matrices, and horizontal braces are Inner.

_layout_children! inserts the space between adjacent atoms from _SPACINGS (Display and Text styles) or _TIGHT_SPACINGS (Script and ScriptScript), in src/tables/layout_spacing.jl. A :bin becomes :ord at the start of a list, after :bin, :op, :rel, :open, or :punct, and before :rel, :close, or :punct (TeX rules 5 and 6; _BIN_LEFT_CANCEL and _BIN_RIGHT_CANCEL).

Styles and sizes (src/style.jl)

TexStyle is TeX's eight styles, numbered so that each cramped style is the even number after its uncramped one, which makes the transitions tuple look-ups:

FunctionGivesUsed for
sup_style(s)Script or ScriptScript, cramped if s issuperscripts
sub_style(s)CrampedScript or CrampedScriptScriptsubscripts
frac_num_style(s)one level down, cramped if s isnumerators
frac_den_style(s)one level down, crampeddenominators
cramp_style(s)the cramped form of sradicands, accented bases

The transitions follow Appendix G of the TeXbook, checked against KaTeX's Style.ts. size_scale(s, mc) is 1 in Display and Text and the font's script_percent_scale_down and script_script_percent_scale_down in the script styles; no scale is hard-coded. A size switch (\large) multiplies the scale, and _scale_for_child carries it through style changes:

child_scale = parent_scale × size_scale(child_style) / size_scale(parent_style)

Constructs

Scripts (src/layout/scripts.jl). _layout_superscript!, _layout_subscript!, and _layout_decorated! follow TeX rule 18 with the MATH constants superscript_shift_up (and its cramped form), superscript_bottom_min, superscript_baseline_drop_max, subscript_shift_down, subscript_top_max, subscript_baseline_drop_min, sub_superscript_gap_min, and superscript_bottom_max_with_subscript. A subscript on a character or a large operator moves left by the base's italic correction (_subscript_italic_correction_em); the superscript does not. The scripted atom is followed by space_after_script, and hlayout_math counts that space at the end of inline math even though no box carries it.

Limits. _use_limits(base, style) puts scripts above and below the base, centred, for a large operator or an operator in _LIMITS_OPERATORS in Display style, for any base under \limits, and for one under \displaylimits or \operatorname* in Display style; \nolimits always puts them beside it. The gaps come from upper_limit_gap_min, upper_limit_baseline_rise_min, lower_limit_gap_min, and lower_limit_baseline_drop_min.

Fractions (_layout_frac!, rule 15). Numerator and denominator are set in their styles, shifted from the axis by the fraction_numerator_* and fraction_denominator_* constants (display forms in Display style) and pushed apart to the minimum gaps, centred over the wider of the two, with a rule of fraction_rule_thickness on the axis. \binom (_layout_genfrac!) has no rule and uses the stack_* constants, with delimiters of TeX's fixed delim1 and delim2 sizes.

Radicals (_layout_sqrt!, _layout_radical!). The radical sign is the smallest vertical variant that covers the radicand plus radical_vertical_gap (or its display form) and radical_rule_thickness, or an assembly when none is tall enough; the rule sits on its top. A degree is kerned by radical_kern_before_degree and radical_kern_after_degree and raised by radical_degree_bottom_raise_percent of the sign's height, measured from the bottom of the sign, as LuaTeX does.

Delimiters. _layout_delimited! sets the inner list, then _layout_delim! picks for each side the smallest vertical variant that meets TeX's rule 19 size (_DELIMITER_FACTOR and _DELIMITER_SHORTFALL), or an assembly, centred on the axis. \middle delimiters take the size of their pair. A null delimiter (\left.) is _NULL_DELIMITER_SPACE wide. \big and its relatives (_layout_big_delim!) take a fixed height from _BIG_DELIM_HEIGHTS.

Extensible assemblies (src/layout/extensible.jl). _layout_assembly! builds tall delimiters, wide accents, horizontal braces, and extensible arrows from the font's assembly parts: the fewest extender repetitions that reach the target, with the overlap at each joint shared evenly between min_connector_overlap and the shorter connector (_assembly_overlaps). Radicals have their own assembly (_layout_radical_assembly!). Arrows that the font has no construction for are built from pieces (_XARROW_FILL_PIECES).

Accents (rule 12). The accent is placed over the base's top-accent attachment point and raised by the base's height above accent_base_height (TeX's δ), so it sits on an x-height base and rides higher over ascenders; the base is set cramped, and scripts attach to the accented character. Wide accents stretch through the horizontal constructions. \overline and \underline add a rule at the overbar_* and underbar_* gaps.

Font switches. Inside \mathbf and the other alphabets, _variant_glyph maps each character with _math_variant_codepoint (src/tables/math_alphabets.jl) to the Mathematical Alphanumeric Symbols block, or to the Letterlike Symbols that Unicode encodes earlier (ℂ, ℌ, ℎ, …). Math alphabets stay in the math font: the text slots of a FontFamily are used by documents and \text{…}, not by \mathbf.

Matrices and alignments (src/layout/matrix.jl)

Matrix-like environments share one grid layout, spaced as LaTeX with amsmath does it (_MATRIX_SPACING in src/tables/layout_spacing.jl): column separation (\arraycolsep and its amsmath forms), \arraystretch struts in every row, \doublerulesep for ||, and for smallmatrix script-style entries with its own baseline skip. Widths, heights, and depths agree with LuaLaTeX to 0.001 pt in the cases measured. Delimited environments (pmatrix, cases, …) size their delimiters against the whole box by rule 19.

\\[len] row spacing follows LaTeX: in an array a positive length deepens the row to at least the strut depth plus the length; elsewhere it adds space. Lengths use TeX units at 10 pt = 1 em; register lengths such as \jot count as zero.

The display alignments align, aligned, split, gather, and gathered use the same grid with amsmath's aligned and gathered spacing: a \strut@ in every row, \openup\jot between rows, and \minalignsep between column pairs. A cell after & starts with an empty ordinary atom, so a leading = gets relation spacing. Their cells are set in Display style (_DISPLAY_MATH_ENVS); matrix, array, and cases stay in Text style, and smallmatrix in script style, as in TeX. There is no display width, so align does not spread its column pairs across the line, and nothing is numbered. equation is a plain display, the same as \[…\]. A starred name is an alias of the unstarred one (_canonical_env_name).

multline is not implemented: it has no alignment points and needs a width-aware path. See future.md for this and the other gaps (\hline, @{…}, \\*, [t] and [b], \strut, and the phantoms).
